## Day 2 - Collaboration, Stories and Storytelling
MVP: 

- Def 1 2001 (Frank Robinson): Smallest successful release that can be successful (Minimum: Small, Viable: meet business needs = successful, Product = )

- Def 2 2011 (Eric Ries): Next best test Smallest thing to we can develop to test a product hypothesis (Minimum: Small, Viable: , Product = Test)

- Def 3 (most common): First crappy release or most we can build that stakeholders approve 

Validated Learning Cycle (faster than sprints, hours/days at worst weeks):

- Build → Measure → Learn → Build ([Lean Startup by Eric Ries](https://www.thalia.de/shop/home/artikeldetails/A1034565509),[ Toyota Way](https://www.thalia.de/shop/home/artikeldetails/A1060714409) (origin of Lean thinking, not Lean Six Sigma)

## Day 3
Mission (public) = always relevant, pupose

Vision (normally internal) = 5+ years into the future, not accurate specification but rather target - is ambitious, futuristic and farfetched, unbelievable. Purpose is to inspire and align the people working on product.

Strategy (internal)

- shortterm→ monthly/quarterly max. 1-2 years - describes step-wise approach to achieve vision and how the org will be sustained grow, who will be helped and when?

- Roadmaps are not strategic - just a list of features we do x in Q1… → the why is important (outcome oriented Roadmaps)

Alan Cooper: the reason we use user personas in software

## Day 4
What helped in our setup was to first split 
KRIs and KPIs. KRIs = everything that cannot be connected to one team / initiative but many teams / initiatives. Example: revenue. KPI is something that can be targeted at a specific team. What a team can influence – metrics to measure the output of a team. This we distinguish from OKRs that are connected to outcome metrics and impact

Teresa Torres for product discovery (also has webinars) 

Would you bet X it would work (depending on stage)?
